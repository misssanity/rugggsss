How it works

A real rug.
For your desktop.

Rugggsss is a tiny Mac app I built for myself because my desktop was a disaster. It lies quietly on top of your icon clutter, so you can stop looking at it.

draped-over-my-mess.pngOpen with Rugggsss

Real cloth physics

Grab it anywhere and it lifts, folds, drags, drops and settles. It drapes over your actual desktop icons, so they show up as bumps. Bigger piles, bigger lumps.

Double-click to roll it up and peek underneath. Double-click again to unroll.

drag-and-roll.mov
Drag it. Double-click to roll it up, and again to unroll.

Stays out of your way

  • Lightweight. It sits quietly behind your app windows.
  • Menu bar icon, right-click menu and a big-button Rug Settings window.
  • Remembers your rug, and can open at login.

Your files are safe.

Rugggsss never opens, moves or changes your files. The Finder permission is only used to learn where your icons are.

How to open Rugggsss

Rugggsss is free and isn't notarized by Apple, so the first time you open it, macOS will say it can't verify the app. That's expected. Here's the quick fix.

  • Open Rugggsss.dmg from your Downloads.
  • Drag Rugggsss onto the Applications folder in that window.
  • Double-click it. You'll see the warning; click Done.
  • Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  • Scroll down and click Open Anyway next to Rugggsss, then confirm with your password or Touch ID.
  • When it asks to control Finder, click Allow. That's how it knows where your icons are, so it can make the bumps. It works without it too, just flatter.

You only do this once. After that, it's just you and your rug.

Will it delete my files?

No. Rugggsss never opens, moves or changes your files. It only asks Finder where your icons are, so it can drape over them. Your mess is safe under there.

Will it get in my way?

No. It's lightweight, and it sits quietly behind your app windows.

How do I find something under the rug?

Double-click to roll it up and peek. Double-click again to unroll.

What do I need?

A Mac with Apple Silicon, running a recent version of macOS.
